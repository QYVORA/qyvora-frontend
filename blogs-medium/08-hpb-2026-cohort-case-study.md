The Hacker Protocol Bootcamp (HPB) **2026 Cohort** was more than just a training program. It was a **mission**. We set out to find, train, and credential the next generation of offensive security operators in Africa, and what we accomplished exceeded every expectation.

Over the course of the bootcamp, participants moved through five intensive phases: from the hacker mindset all the way to advanced social engineering, completing **20 rooms** and earning **on-chain verified CyberPoints (CP)**. But the real story isn't the curriculum. It's the **community, the outcomes, and the team** that emerged from this cohort.

**Note on our evolution:** When the HPB 2026 Cohort launched, we operated under the name **HSOCIETY OFFSEC**. Since then, we have evolved into **QYVORA**, a unified platform for offensive security training, credentialing, and operations. The bootcamp's results and participants remain the same; only the banner has grown.

**The Five-Phase Pipeline**

The HPB curriculum was structured as a **progressive pipeline**. Each phase built on the previous one, ensuring participants developed deep, operational fluency before moving to the next layer.

Phase 01: Hacker Mindset
Phase 02: Linux Foundations
Phase 03: Networking & Protocol Analysis
Phase 04: Web & Backend Systems
Phase 05: Social Engineering & Operational Security

**Phase 01 - Hacker Mindset:** Participants learned the ethical and legal frameworks that distinguish security researchers from malicious actors. Scope definition, rules of engagement, and the operational discipline required to operate professionally.

**Phase 02 - Linux Foundations:** Terminal fluency was built from the ground up, filesystem navigation, privilege escalation, process management, and bash scripting. No graphical interfaces, no shortcuts.

**Phase 03 - Networking:** Total visibility over the network stack. TCP/IP, OSI layers, packet interception with tcpdump and Wireshark, DNS interrogation, and Nmap mastery.

**Phase 04 - Web & Backend Systems:** The modern attack surface. Participants dissected HTTP, exploited SQL injection and XSS, manipulated authentication flows, and used Burp Suite for web application testing.

**Phase 05 - Social Engineering:** The human layer. Pretexting, phishing, OSINT, and physical security concepts including tailgating and RFID cloning.

(Screenshot: Hands-on terminal exercise during the Linux Foundations phase, participants navigating real command-line environments.)

**Beyond the Core Curriculum**

While the five phases formed the backbone of the bootcamp, we brought in **real-world tools and scenarios** that gave participants exposure to the actual toolkit of a professional offensive security operator.

- **IP Geolocation** -- Participants learned to trace IP addresses to physical locations, analyse ISP ownership, and correlate geolocation data with threat intelligence feeds.
- **Shodan** -- Deep dive into Shodan for internet-wide device discovery, identifying exposed industrial control systems, open databases, and misconfigured servers.
- **CTF Competition** -- A capture-the-flag competition where participants applied everything they learned in a timed, competitive environment with real attack scenarios.

We also held an **open Q&A session** where participants could ask anything about careers in cybersecurity, the tools we use daily, and the realities of working in offensive security. The engagement was intense, participants asked questions for over two hours straight.

**How It Worked Under the Hood**

The HPB ran on QYVORA's **interactive room architecture**, a terminal-first, browser-based learning environment where every concept was taught through structured walkthroughs with built-in quiz validation. No local setup, no dependency management, no friction.

Each room completion triggered a **blockchain transaction** on our Proof-of-Authority chain:

```
Student -> Complete Room -> Solve Quiz
              |
   Proof-of-Authority Validator
              |
     CyberPoints (CP) Minted On-Chain
              |
     Verifiable Credential -> CV / Portfolio
```

This architecture ensures that every credential is **portable and permanent**. Even if QYVORA's platform changes, the on-chain record remains.
